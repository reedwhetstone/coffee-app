# Outline: How Would You Know a Great Coffee Before You Roast It?

**Pillar:** market-intelligence
**Target:** about 1,300 words plus three charts
**Status:** drafted (revision 2)
**Source material:** Reed's concept note and feedback (#blog-post-context-is-everything, 2026-10-02); `notes/research/producer-track-record/`

## Thesis

Nobody in specialty coffee can reliably say what identifies a quality coffee before it is roasted. Its quality signals are inconsistent across sellers, often unverifiable or false, and forgotten after every harvest. Buffett trusted long records that he could check. Wine found a legitimate way to store reputation (enforced identity plus a public record), so its prices learned to follow quality. Coffee pays for identity but throws it away, and its one long public record, Cup of Excellence, shows that quality persists weakly and is priced only at the top.

## Structure

1. Opening: score explains about 0.5% of retail price and country explains 43%; buyers discount signals for good reason.
2. The signals don't travel: chart of supplier-relative scores; hard versus soft information; fruit descriptors; Kona, Gesha DNA and the eco-label bias; chart of flat price across score bands.
3. Buffett trusted records he could check: acquisition criteria, See's Candies, Frazzini/Kabiller/Pedersen.
4. Wine made reputation usable: fraud led to appellations; Combris; Oczkowski; coffee processing experiments weaken place as a predictor of the cup.
5. Coffee pays for identity, then forgets it: catalog identity coverage; a hedged identity premium; Ethiopia ECX.
6. The one long record: Cup of Excellence persistence study and an auction premium chart.
7. What a usable record needs: identity, blind sensory evidence on a known scale, and claims with source and corroboration; close by resolving the opening statistic.

## Decisions

- Fantasy football analogy dropped as too corny (Reed).
- Personal anecdote removed (Reed: it undercut authority). The post opens on market data.
- Wine framing: wine found a legitimate way to store reputation for buying decisions, not "wine did it first" (Reed).
- Unreliable metadata (Kona, Gesha) is central to the argument, not an aside (Reed).
- Suppliers are anonymized in charts.
- The identity price premium is reported as directional only. With standard errors clustered by supplier, the 14.5% estimate has p = 0.04, a 95% CI of about 1% to 30%, and about 93 listings with identity.
- Claims to avoid: that direct trade raises farm-gate prices, that named producers raise consumer willingness to pay, or that wine labels guarantee quality.
- The lot-level Cup of Excellence dataset stays out of the public repo until republishing rights are settled.
